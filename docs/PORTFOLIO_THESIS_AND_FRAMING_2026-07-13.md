# Portfolio North Star — how to look at this correctly

- **Date:** 2026-07-13
- **Author:** Claude (research lane), with the operator. Educational / decision-
  support, not financial advice.
- **Purpose:** step back from the tools and name what this portfolio *is*, what the
  real mission is, and the blind spots to hold in mind. The tracker/alerts/composite
  system is the *how*; this is the *why* and the *guardrails*.

## What this portfolio actually is

A **concentrated, high-conviction, single-theme bet** on the AI / data-center /
hyperscaler / robotics capex supercycle — expressed through ~30 names that are
mostly the *same underlying bet*:

| Underlying factor | Share of basket |
|---|---|
| Semiconductors (chips) | ~30% |
| Semiconductor-adjacent hardware (storage, servers, optical, networking) | ~30% |
| Data-center infrastructure (power/cooling, operators) | ~13% |
| Mega-cap tech / hyperscalers | ~7% |
| Software (cloud rails, security) | ~10% |
| Industrials (bearings) — the only real diversifier | ~10% |

**~80% of the book rides the semiconductor / AI-capex cycle.** Thirty tickers, but
functionally ~1.5 real bets. Proof, live: on 2026-07-13 the broad market was
−0.5%, SMH −3%, and these names −5 to −7% — they move together, amplified. Owning
*more* AI names is not diversification; it is more of the same wager.

This is a legitimate way to express strong conviction. It just must be seen for
what it is — a leveraged thematic bet — not mistaken for a diversified portfolio.

## The philosophy fork: momentum vs "buy low" (resolve this)

The stated instinct is "buy low, sell high." **Everything built here does the
opposite.** The tracker, momentum ranking, and composite tags **buy strength and
cut weakness** — they flag beaten-down names (ORCL −61%) as AVOID, not as dips to
buy. That is *momentum*: ride winners, exit losers, never catch a falling knife.

Momentum and "buy the dip" contradict — you can't run both on the same name at
once. **Pick the lane.** For a concentrated thematic bull position, momentum /
trend discipline is the more defensible choice, and it is what the system
implements. If the gut says "buy low," it will fight the tools by buying the exact
names they say to avoid. Decide consciously which trader you are.

## The mission, reframed honestly

"Buy low, sell high, buy cabins in the mountains" is the dream, not an operational
mission — no one reliably nails tops and bottoms. The achievable mission underneath
it:

1. **Express the AI-buildout conviction** (done — the basket is the bet).
2. **Don't give it all back** when the theme corrects — step aside at the trend
   break instead of round-tripping the run. (This is the tracker/alerts' job.)
3. **Size it to survive** a semiconductor bear market (chips can draw down 40-60%
   peak-to-trough). Concentration + leverage-by-correlation means a theme unwind
   hits ~80% of the book at once.

The cabins come from **compounding without ruin**, not from perfect timing.

## Are we looking at it correctly? Mostly — with two blind spots

**Right:** trend discipline is the correct overlay for a concentrated thematic bet.
The system rides the theme while it works and flags the exit when the 200-day
breaks, which is exactly how you avoid round-tripping a crowded trade.

**Blind spot 1 — the concentration illusion.** 30 names feel diversified; they
aren't (~1.5 bets). Know your true exposure. The bearings/software names are the
only real diversifiers; consider whether you want more genuinely uncorrelated
ballast.

**Blind spot 2 — the thesis is not the edge.** "AI data centers" is the most
consensus trade in the market; everyone owns it. Being bullish the obvious is not
an advantage. **Your only durable edge is the discipline** — the mechanical exit
rule that gets you out at the trend break when this crowded trade eventually
unwinds. The system's value is not finding the thesis (you have it); it's keeping
you from being the last one holding it.

## The one-line north star

*A concentrated, high-conviction momentum bet on the AI-capex supercycle — where the
edge is not the thesis (everyone has it) but the discipline to ride the trend and
step aside at the break, sized so a semiconductor bear market can't take the account
with it.*

## Honest caveats

- This is not financial advice; it's a framing of the approach you're running.
- Concentration cuts both ways: it amplifies the upside you're betting on and the
  drawdown you're exposed to. The trend overlay manages *timing*, not the fact that
  this is a single-factor bet.
- At a small account, position size and staying un-wiped-out matter more than any
  ranking refinement — same conclusion as every other branch of this research.
