three to five years for ownership, with separately budgeted shorter trades.

The opportunity is substantial spending on compute and physical infrastructure.
The investment question is which shareholders retain the economics, at what
purchase price, and how long the position can wait. Treat every name as a
candidate with a falsifiable thesis. A conviction list is not a list of assured
stock winners.

## Choose the clock before choosing the instrument

| Lane | Research horizon | Entry work | Addition or exit logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | 3–5 years, quarterly thesis reviews | Public operating evidence, conservative financing and dilution cases, valuation under slower growth | Add when per-share economics improve or valuation offers more room with thesis intact; reconsider on thesis failure or poor prospective return |
| Business-milestone trade | Roughly 3–12 months | Dated commissioning, product ramp or funding milestone, plus valuation and price response | Reassess after delivery, delay or repricing; define in advance whether any remainder becomes ownership |
| Event trade | Days to weeks | Confirmed event date, actual option chain, net payoff after spread/fees, explicit loss amount | Manage the contract deadline and remaining upside; do not extend a failed short trade merely by relabeling it long term |

Shares generally fit uncertain multi-quarter delivery better than an expiring
option. An option needs a thesis about *when* and *how far* the stock moves that
survives the premium paid. Long-dated options still have expiration, volatility
and capital-cost tradeoffs. A cheap premium is not automatically good asymmetry.

For ownership, a generic tight percentage stop can force repeated exits during
normal volatility. Test wider, thesis-based holding rules by reducing initial
dollars and recording adverse excursions; do not simply widen a stop while
keeping the same dollars at risk. A stop order cannot guarantee its trigger price.

## Size the account, the shared theme and the position

There are three separate quantities: maximum committed capital, a loss under a
specified stress scenario, and maximum contractual loss for an intact option
structure. A portfolio can have several tickers but one dominant economic risk.
[FINRA's concentration guidance](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/concentration-risk)
explains why correlated industry exposure must be assessed across positions.

For shares, use `position dollars = account loss dollars / assumed decline` as
a scenario tool. A 35% decline is a stress assumption, not a maximum possible
loss or an empirically calibrated stop. Add the losses across shared exposure
before funding another position. For debit spreads, use the complete paired
debit and payoff, while separately managing expiration/assignment mechanics.

The accompanying calculation compares 2%, 5% and 10% account-loss scenarios
against 20%, 35% and 50% stock declines. These numbers illustrate tradeoffs;
none changes an enforced cap. In particular, 10% on several correlated ideas
is a much larger portfolio bet than 10% once. Do not maximize every row.

Stage capital according to improved evidence. A starter establishes exposure;
a later tranche follows verified execution or a better valuation with the
thesis intact; the remaining capacity is optional. The allocator's existing
40/35/25 tranche pattern is a candidate to evaluate, not a reason to average
down automatically. A falling price without better economics does not earn
the next tranche.

Avoid selling a durable winner simply because it gained 3–5%. Review ownership
when valuation outruns plausible per-share cash generation, weight exceeds the
operator's chosen allocation, or the thesis deteriorates. Event spreads have
a different tradeoff: little remaining payoff near maximum value can make
continuing to carry expiration exposure unattractive. There is no universal
profit-taking percentage for both lanes.

## Immediate plan and funding sequence

**September 11:** reconcile and manage the existing ORCL spread first. Use a
current executable spread market, set an acceptable closing limit and verify
the whole-position fill. Avoid planning around an assumed profit. If held into
expiration, understand broker exercise/assignment procedures and deadlines;
near the short strike the final stock position can be uncertain.
[OIC bull-call-spread mechanics](https://www.optionseducation.org/strategies/all-strategies/bull-call-spread-debit-call-spread).

Confirm any scheduled deposit in the account before treating it as funds. Cash,
settled/deployable funds, unrealized marks and realized option proceeds remain
separate. The existing allocator's 25% options / 50% ownership / 25% reserve
layout is an unvalidated planning split, not permission to spend every sleeve.
This research does not require two new tickets tomorrow.

**Next funding decision:** compare one supplier/infrastructure ownership
candidate with adding to one current development position. Establish valuation
cases first. Until then, retaining funding capacity is a legitimate decision.
Use confirmed deposits to build the desired exposure gradually; do not require
an option win to finance the next trade. Avoid adding to all correlated holdings
merely because their prices are below cost basis.

**Weekly review:** price response, event-date changes, revised guidance, funding
announcements and concentration. **Quarterly review:** delivered capacity,
utilization, realized pricing, margins, cash generation, debt and fully diluted
share count. **Annual review:** benchmark-relative results after contributions,
withdrawals, costs and taxes where available; which decisions added value.
These are review instructions, not a newly scheduled automation.

## What remains unproven

This pass supplies account reconciliation, stress math, dated price context and
issuer-specific milestone cards. It does not supply audited forward consensus,
a current valuation model for every candidate, a validated optimal allocation,
or evidence that loosening technical gates improves net outcomes.

Before adopting a challenger, freeze its holding horizon, entry/add/exit rules,
capital assumptions and falsifier. Compare the present method with ownership
tranches on the same candidates using later observations; include rejected
names, drawdowns, turnover and costs. Do not tune thresholds on this small
selected snapshot. Keep research success separate from paper and live profits.
